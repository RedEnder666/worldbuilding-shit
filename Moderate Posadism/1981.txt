The Domino Falls

“…and with her died my last warm feelings for humanity.”
— J.V. Stalin, 1932

# Stalin's worst nightmare. Reformism.

**1962:** Argentine Trotskyist visionary J. Posadas together with his lieutenant and good friend Dante Minazzoli publish the Posadist Manifesto. It calls for a collective liberation of all knowledge - total abolition of all "intellectual property" along with the private property, but also formulates a materialist analysis of the UFO phenomena, concluding that interstellar travel is impossible in scarcity, and therefore, all alien life forms visiting the planet are communist by definition. He argued that we should accelerate the humanity's technological progress in space travel in order to be able to reach or communicate to the alien life forms.

**1963:** 
- Leonid Brezhnev chokes to death during a New Year's breakfast. The conservative coup against Nikita Khrushchev falters before it begins.
- On November 22, in Dallas, Texas, a sequence of shots ring out from the Texas School Book Depository as President Kennedy's motorcade passes. History fractures on a centimeter's difference. The first shot misses. The second, fired as the limousine turns onto Elm Street, strikes the President in the upper right shoulder, passing through without hitting bone or major arteries. A third shot goes astray. President survives. After emergency surgery, a pale and pain-etched John F. Kennedy appears before the nation from his hospital room. His voice is weakened but his eyes hold a fire no one had seen before. He states, calmly, that the problem is not the left or the right - it is the radicalism that has poisoned both. And he will fight for human rights against all radicalism - right and left.

**1964:** 
- In the US, the Civil Rights Act passes. Kennedy argues "This law is the antidote to the radical poison. It is the rational, just solution that denies extremists on all sides the chaos they feed on."

**1965-1970:** The **Khrushchev-Kosygin Continuum** solidifies. De-Stalinization becomes irreversible. The Kosygin Reforms transform the Soviet economy into a hybrid model of planned efficiency and market-style responsiveness. "Peaceful Coexistence" is a doctrine of confident competition, not weakness.

**1971:** An aging Khrushchev retires with state honors. **Alexei Kosygin** assumes the role of General Secretary. He appoints the young reformer **Mikhail Gorbachev** as Premier.

1972: The ideology of Posadism resonates deeply with the Aymara and Quechua miners. The POR-TP (Partido Obrero Revolucionario Trotskista Posadista) transforms from a small Trotskyist party into a mass Indigenous-Posadist Movement.

1973: In Chile, General Augusto Pinochet perishes in a freak helicopter accident during his own coup attempt. The socialist government of Salvador Allende survives, stabilizes, and becomes a democratic socialist beacon.

1968-1977: The Mexican Civil War. Government collapse leads to a three-way fracture:

The Mexican Free State, a US-backed fascist rump in the north.

The Republic of Mexico, a fragile, neutral remnant clinging to Mexico City.

The Mayab Minds' Republic, a Trotskyist-Posadist state born from an alliance of Maya rebels and intellectuals in the south, preaching the liberation of knowledge and cosmic outreach. Their heroes are Zapata, Trotsky, Posadas, and various ufologists.

1976: The International Lunar Expedition. What was once a race becomes a partnership. The United States and the Soviet Union, building on the goodwill of Apollo-Soyuz, announce a joint mission to the Moon. The world holds its breath.

On July 20, 1976—exactly seven years after Apollo 11—the Unity lander touches down in the Sea of Tranquility. Aboard are four souls: American commanders David Scott and Vance Brand, and Soviet cosmonauts Alexei Leonov and Oleg Makarov. When Scott plants a flag, it is not the Stars and Stripes alone, but the flag of the United Nations beside it. Leonov places a banner from the Soviet Space Program nearby. Between them, a small plaque reads: "We came as rivals. We return as partners. For all humanity."

The image circles the globe: four figures in their distinct but complementary spacesuits, helmets touching in a gesture of shared wonder. The Cold War, in that moment, becomes something else entirely—a competition not of destruction, but of cooperation. The "Moon Race" ends not with a victor, but with a handshake.

1977-1979: In Bolivia, a brutal government crackdown on a miners' strike in 1977 sparks a nationwide uprising. The POR-TP, now allied with militant campesino militias and radicalized factions of the military, inspired by the Mayab Minds' Republic, launches a coordinated insurrection. The People's Assembly of Workers, Peasants, and Soldiers, dominated by the POR-TP, declares the Socialist Republic of the Andean Cosmos (República Socialista del Cosmos Andino).

1978: The Kennedy-Kosygin Summit in Reykjavik produces the Reykjavik Accords. Both superpowers agree to: joint moratorium on anti-satellite weapons testing; shared scientific data from all lunar and planetary missions; and the creation of a joint working group on a permanent space station. The world begins to breathe easier.

1979: The USSR, pragmatic and wary of quagmires, opts for massive proxy support to the Parcham faction in Afghanistan's civil war. It does not invade. The Cold War gains a new, static frontline - but one fought with arms and aid, not occupation.


**1980:** 
- A Mayab astrophysicist, aided by Soviet *Interkosmos* program, becomes the Republic's first citizen in space. The launch is a ideological thunderclap. A citizen of a tiny, unrecognized republic, orbiting the Earth.
- USSR, under the GLASNOST policy of Kosygin and Gorbachev, publicly states the development of the OGAS network, using the Akademset infrastructure, is already in progress. "In the end, communism is about precision. OGAS provides that precision. It replaces the fog of the plan with the clarity of the algorithm." — General Secretary Kosygin.
- The Soviet Space Program is rebranded SOVKOSMOS in the pages of Pravda, but in practice, it functions increasingly as the eastern pillar of an informal global scientific partnership. American and Soviet engineers now attend each other's conferences. Student exchanges are common.

**1981:**
- "We will not match their five-year plans with our own. We will drown their network with a thousand—no, a million of our own making, in garages, in universities, in free association." — President Frank Forrester Church III. But his words ring somewhat hollow. The "Cold Race" is no longer about dominance; it is about who can inspire the world more.
- The Cold War turns into "Cold Race" - the superpowers trying to one-up eachother in progress. International Pact is an interesting, but minor faction, seen as a poison for the Moscow's well.
- "We're looking at the one place that hasn't been corrupted by capitalism... SPACE!" — Head of Sovkosmos, Anatoly Cherdenko, during an interview. He was shortly corrected by a party representative, and continued: "Obviously, thanks to our comrades from America, who supported the soviet people since 1917!"
- The International Pact—a loose coalition of non-aligned and socialist states—remains a minor faction, but its influence grows as the superpowers' cooperation makes ideological purity harder to maintain. The Mayab Minds' Republic and the Andean Cosmos find themselves increasingly courted by both Moscow and Washington, each seeking allies in a world where the old certainties are dissolving.
