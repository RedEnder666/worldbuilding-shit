**Theopsychosis** (from Greek: _theos_, "god" + _psyche_, "mind/soul" + _-osis_, "condition"), formerly a folk diagnosis, is a clinically recognized sub-type of Complex Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD-C). It is characterized by a profound existential crisis and subsequent identity restructuring, triggered by the sudden collapse of a foundational theocratic worldview.

### History and Classification

The term "theopsychosis" originated as a colloquialism among refugees and dissidents who fled or survived the fall of the Sechian Empire. It described the radical personal transformations observed in those who had undergone the "Shattering"—the realization that the divine framework governing their entire life was a lie.

Following the Sechian Civil War (1901-1911), the condition was formally classified by the **ICWP Bureau of Public Health** in 1924. Its clinical definition codified this folk understanding, naming it **Post-Theocratic Stress Complex (PTSC)** while acknowledging "theopsychosis" as its common name.

### Clinical Definition

According to the *ICWP-BPH Diagnostic Manual of Psychic Injuries (5th Edition)*, diagnostic criteria for Theopsychosis (PTSC) include:

- A history of indoctrination within and subsequent liberation from a totalitarian theocratic state.
    
- The core trauma: The "Shattering," a catastrophic disintegration of the foundational theocratic worldview, resulting in a profound dissolution of personal identity and meaning.
    
- The primary symptom: **Identity Hyper-Fixation.** The individual's psyche seizes upon a single concept, pursuit, or ideology—which can range from the mundane to the profound—and restructures their entire identity and life around it with monomaniacal intensity. This new "anchor" serves as a compensatory foundation for a shattered self, and its specific nature is less significant than the totalizing, all-consuming quality of the commitment.
