The **Commons Coordination Board** is the primary legislative and administrative body of the [[Union of Societist Communes of Entantha]]. As of the 2024 General Allocation, it comprises 70 seats distributed among various political parties and coalitions. The Board convenes in the Hall of Communes in Arsis, Entantha.

## Current Composition (2024-2028 Term)

![[24-28 board.svg]]

### Governing Blocs & Major Parties

#### Anarcho-Cooperativist Party (ACP) - 25 Seats
*Ideology: "No gods, no centralization."*
- **Status:** Largest Bloc (Plurality)
- **Real Power:** Possesses de facto veto power over any attempt at state centralization. They are the primary reason the Commons Board remains a lawmaking body rather than evolving into a traditional executive government.
- **2025 Platform:** Strengthening local commune autonomy, expanding cooperative legal protections, and preventing the emergence of any permanent bureaucratic class.

#### Democratic-Societist (DSP) & Socialist Democratic (SDP) Coalition - 24 Seats
*Ideology: "Art, drugs, vibes, and automated luxury are human rights."*
- **Role:** Traditionally the champions of cultural production and personal freedoms. In the 2020s, they have become the leading advocates for technological liberation.
- **2025 Platform:** The coalition's primary legislative push is the **"Automation, Stupid" Act**, which advocates for the full automation of all tedious, dangerous, or repetitive labor ("Stupid Labor") via state-developed AI and robotics. They argue this is the logical endpoint of societist principles, freeing human potential for creative, social, and intellectual pursuits.
- **Public Perception:** Widely popular and seen as the "heart" of the USCE's cultural policy.

#### Liberal-Societist (LSP) & SocDem (SocDP) Coalition - 14 Seats
*Ideology: "Markets can be fun if nobody starves!"*
- **Role:** Advocates for market-mechanisms within the discretionary sector.
- **2025 Platform:** Supports the DSP-SDP's automation drive, seeing it as a way to boost productivity and expand the pool of discretionary points available for trade. They continue to push for the abolition of the "Body and Behavioral Stability Goods" category, seeking to fold it into the general Discretionary Goods system.
- **Alliance:** Often forms a working majority with the DSP-SDP on automation-related bills.

#### Party of The Military Servicemen and Patriotic Duty (MSPD) - 4 Seats
*Ideology: "We won the revolution, now give us nice parks, public transit, and reliable infrastructure."*
- **Role:** The veterans' party, focused overwhelmingly on quality-of-life and urban planning.
- **2025 Platform:** "Green Zones for All." They support automation if it leads to better-maintained public works. Their focus remains on ensuring every street has its mandated five green zones.

### Smaller Parties & Factions

#### Automation Solidarity Front (ASF) - 2 Seats
*Ideology: "From each according to their ability, to each according to their needs, with the robots doing the rest."*
- **A recent splinter group** from the DSP's more techno-optimist wing. They are more radical than their parent party, advocating for the immediate dissolution of all non-creative job quotas and the establishment of a Universal Basic Point Allocation funded entirely by automated production.
- **Relationship:** Critical allies of the DSP-SDP on automation, but push for faster, more comprehensive implementation.

#### The wENDesday Party - 1 Seat
*Ideology: "Wednesday is the new Friday. And Monday is still the worst."*
- **Status:** Gained its first seat in the 2024 allocation, a testament to its growing cultural cachet.
- **Platform:** Officially, they campaign for a mid-week "weekbreak" to combat burnout and boost morale. Unofficially, their rallies are massive, festive block parties that have become a significant part of urban youth culture.

#### Other Registered Political Organizations
A number of registered political organizations maintain active membership and participate in public discourse, though they did not meet the electoral threshold for representation in the current Board. These groups represent a spectrum of ideological and single-issue interests within the Union's political landscape.

- **Guardist Party:** A Vanguardist Socialist organization that critiques the current USCE model as a deviation from revolutionary principles. It argues that the focus on cooperatives and automation constitutes an abandonment of the proletarian vanguard role.
- **Party of Anarchists of Entantha:** An organization advocating for the complete dissolution of all formal governance structures, including the Commons Coordination Board. It distinguishes itself from the Anarcho-Cooperativist Party by its rejection of any form of structured political participation.
- **Robotics Citizenship Party:** A single-issue organization that campaigns for the legal recognition of personhood and citizenship rights for advanced artificial intelligence and automated systems.
- **The "We Hate Mondays" Faction:** A cultural and political group known for its weekly public gatherings that protest the concept of "Monday". While lacking a formal legislative platform, the faction maintains a consistent presence in public spaces and holds weekly festivals since 2011.

## Function and Power

The Board's primary role is to draft and approve the regulations that frame USCE society, such as the [[Resource Allocation Regulation]] and the [[Cooperatives Regulation]]. Its power is intentionally limited by the foundational principles of the Union; it cannot easily centralize authority or create a permanent standing government, largely due to the structural influence of the ACP. It acts as a coordinator between communes and a resolver of edge-case disputes, rather than a traditional ruling government.
